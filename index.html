Online shopping

An essay

Online shopping: easy to love, worth shopping carefully

“

Online shopping has become the first place many of us look when we need something, whether it is a notebook, a pair of earphones, or a new skincare product. It is quick and often cheaper, but a screen can only tell us so much. Here is how I weigh the good and the bad.

The good side

  1. Everything in one place.

    A single app can show hundreds of options for the same product, so it takes minutes, not an afternoon, to find what fits a budget.

  2. Reviews you can learn from.

    Photos, ratings, and long reviews from real buyers help you spot problems before you pay, which is especially useful for products you cannot try first.

  3. Deals and vouchers.

    Discounts, free shipping, and bundles are easy to find, and a patient shopper can save a meaningful amount.

  4. Reach beyond the neighbourhood.

    Smaller brands and specialty products that no local store stocks are suddenly one tap away, and they arrive at the door.

The not so good side

  1. You cannot test the product.

    Texture, scent, shade, and size can all be different from the photos. For skincare this matters most, because skin reacts differently from person to person.

  2. Fake or mislabelled items.

    Counterfeit products exist, and a very low price should raise a question. Official stores, a registration number from BPOM, and a clear expiry date are easy checks.

  3. Impulse buying.

    Flash sales, countdown timers, and “only 2 left” labels push us to decide fast, and the cart fills up with things we did not plan to buy.

  4. Delivery and returns.

    Parcels can leak, get crushed, or sit in the heat, and a refund can take patience and a lot of messages with the seller.

Habits that help

0 of 4 ticked

Used with a little care, online shopping is a great tool. The trick is to let it save us time without letting it decide for us.

Journals

Five papers

Five papers on game development that I read for the group assignment. Each card has a short summary and links to the article, the PDF, and the DOI.

Showing 5 of 5 journals

  1. Unity and GDLC

    Development of a Web-Based First Person Shooter Game Using Unity with the Game Development Life Cycle Method

    IndonesianOriginal title: Pengembangan Game First Person Shooter Berbasis Web Menggunakan Unity dengan Metode Game Development Life Cycle

    Alghivary, M. M., Suharso, A., & Defiyanti, S. (2025). JATI (Jurnal Mahasiswa Teknik Informatika), 9(1), 1152 to 1159.

    The authors develop a browser-based first person shooter in Unity that runs without extra installation, motivated by the limits local developers face: capital, technology access, project management, and marketing. They follow the Game Development Life Cycle (GDLC) through concept and planning, pre-production, production, and testing. Shooting, respawn, and interface features were built in Visual Studio and checked with black-box testing. The authors conclude that GDLC helps resolve technical obstacles and produces a game that fits user needs.

    Article (opens in a new tab)PDF (opens in a new tab)DOI (opens in a new tab)10.36040/jati.v9i1.12643
  2. Unity and GDLC

    Exploration Game Application “Nusa Sang Penjelajah” Using Unity and C#

    IndonesianOriginal title: Aplikasi Permainan Eksplorasi “Nusa Sang Penjelajah” Menggunakan Unity dan C#

    Rachman, A., & Sari, R. N. (2025). Jurnal Minfo Polgan, 14(2), 3626 to 3632.

    A top-down exploration game in the style of Stardew Valley, built around Indonesian culture through folktales, artifacts, and temples. It was developed in Unity and C# with GDLC (planning, production, testing, and release). The authors report an engaging and educational game, and they position it as a way for young people to engage with local heritage.

    Article (opens in a new tab)PDF (opens in a new tab)DOI (opens in a new tab)10.33395/jmp.v14i2.15876
  3. Unity and GDLC

    Development of an Android-Based Platformer Game “Ayo Lawan Virus” Using the Unity Game Engine

    IndonesianOriginal title: Pengembangan Game Platformer “Ayo Lawan Virus” Berbasis Android dengan Menggunakan Game Engine Unity

    Yedithia, F., Sulistyowati, & Rosmiati. (2022). Jurnal Humaniora Teknologi, 8(1), 1 to 9.

    A game on clean and healthy living behavior (PHBS) for children and teenagers, set against the pandemic. The team ran observations and a literature study on viruses and hygiene practices, then produced the artwork with an illustrator. The finished game, “Ayo Lawan Virus” (Let's Fight the Virus), is distributed free to the public as an educational tool.

    Article (opens in a new tab)PDF (opens in a new tab)DOI (opens in a new tab)10.34128/jht.v8i1.101
  4. Procedural generationSurveys

    Procedural Puzzle Generation: A Survey

    De Kegel, B., & Haahr, M. (2020). IEEE Transactions on Games, 12(1), 21 to 40.

    Procedural content generation (PCG) is used far less for puzzles than for other game elements, and most work stays within pure puzzle games rather than puzzles embedded in other genres. The authors survey 32 methods across eleven puzzle categories and define seven characteristics for comparing them, which also point to promising research areas.

    Open-access PDF, about 20 MB.

    Article (opens in a new tab)PDF (opens in a new tab)DOI (opens in a new tab)10.1109/TG.2019.2917792
  5. Procedural generationSurveys

    Intelligent Generation of Graphical Game Assets: A Conceptual Framework and Systematic Review of the State of the Art

    Fukaya, K., et al. (2025). ACM Computing Surveys, 57(5), Article 118.

    Games contain many graphical assets, such as clouds, buildings, and vegetation, that serve no gameplay function, and PCG is widely used to produce them. The review covers 200 papers on graphical asset generation in and beyond games, spanning 21 asset types, nine techniques, and 28 approaches. The GAGeTx framework consolidates the findings to guide the choice and design of asset generators.

    The PDF is the arXiv preprint (arXiv:2311.10129).
